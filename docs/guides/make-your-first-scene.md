# Make your first scene

Four ways in. Three of them work with AI mode off — no key, no network.

Get to them all the same way: **long-press Home** → **Admin** → **Scenes** →
**New Scene**.

---

## Before you choose

The fastest good scene is usually **not** built from nothing. Core-First
already has the high-frequency vocabulary and the category structure. If you
want a scene for a specific session, consider:

1. Admin → Scenes → swipe left on **Core-First** → **Duplicate**
2. Rename the copy, trim what you don't need, add what you do

That takes about two minutes and inherits a sane core. The four routes below
are for when you want something genuinely different.

You'll get to the same place if you simply try to edit Core-First: it is
supplied by us and read-only, so the app offers to make you an editable copy —
"Core-First - My Copy" — and switches the child to it. Either path is fine; the
swipe is just the deliberate version.

---

## Route 1 — Build from Collections · AI mode off

The one to try first. It builds a real multi-page scene out of vocabulary you
already have, instantly, offline.

1. **New Scene** → **No AI — combine packs & classes**
2. Give it a name — "Snack time", "Farm visit"
3. Pick any **Vocabulary Packs** (Farm, Tide Pools, Mealtime, Space, Dinosaurs,
   Vehicles) and any **Word Classes** (actions, feelings, people, food…)
4. **Create**

Each pack and each class you tick becomes its own page, and a home page is
created linking to all of them.

It's blunt — a class page is *every* word of that class, which can be a lot —
but it gets you a working structure in seconds, and trimming is faster than
building. Open the scene, tap a page, and remove what you don't want.

**Trim in bulk, not tile by tile.** In a page, tap the **checkmark-circle** in
the toolbar — or **⋯ → Select Tiles** if the toolbar is crowded — then tap
every tile you want to act on. A bar appears along the bottom with **Move**,
**Conceal** and **Delete**, and a **Select** menu that will take all of a word
class at once ("All feelings"). This is the control people miss, and it turns
trimming a 60-word class page from a chore into about twenty seconds.

Prefer **Conceal** to Delete while you're still deciding: the word keeps its
place on the board and simply isn't available, so nothing else shifts.

## Route 2 — Describe it, and let the AI build it · needs AI mode

**With AI mode off**, this route is visible but inert: the **Describe the
scene** box is there and you can type in it, and **Generate Scene** is
unavailable. That is deliberate — you can see what the app would do, and the
ready-made examples below the box still load instantly without a key. Nothing
is hidden from you for not having one.

Needs AI mode on. Once it is on, generating a scene takes **20–40 seconds**,
and the cost is in the artwork rather than the words: a few cents per new
picture the scene needs, and nothing for words it can reuse from vocabulary you
already have.

**Turning AI mode on the first time is two separate jobs, and only one of them
is ours.** First you set up a billing relationship with OpenAI — an account, a
payment method, and their verification. That is the part that takes real time,
it happens on their site rather than in Blaster, and you do it once. Then you
create a key, paste it into **Admin → Device**, and you are done; that half
takes a minute, and the app walks you through it.

If someone tells you AI mode is a two-minute setup, they are describing the
second job and forgetting the first.

1. **New Scene** → type into **Describe the scene**
2. Be specific about the child and the goal. Compare:
   - ✗ "feelings"
   - ✓ "feelings for a 5-year-old working on frustration vs anger"
3. **Generate Scene**
4. You get a **Scene Preview** — pages, tiles, pictures, navigation, all of it.
   Scroll through the whole thing.
5. **Accept**, **Refine**, or **Cancel**

**Refine** is the underused one. Rather than accepting something that's 80%
right, describe the change — "drop the school pages, add a page about the
dog" — and it revises.

Nothing is created until you tap Accept, and everything is editable afterwards.

**If the scene introduces new words**, the preview says so, and any word the
moderation check flagged is marked. You must resolve those before Accept.

Flagging is not censorship and it is not a block — it is a beat before a word
the AI proposed goes onto a child's board on your authority. Anatomical terms
get flagged precisely because a caregiver may legitimately need them. You
decide; the app just makes sure you saw it. The full account is in
[Adding vocabulary](adding-vocabulary.md).

## Route 3 — Start from an example · AI mode off

**New Scene** → **Start from an example** → pick a ready-made scene → **Load
Example**. Instant, and nothing is generated. Good for seeing what a well-formed scene looks like
before building your own. Edit the description first and the button switches to
Generate, using the example as a starting point.

## Route 4 — Manual · AI mode off

Toolbar → **Manual** → name → **Create**. An empty scene and an editor. Use
this when you know exactly what you want and it's small.

## After it exists

You land in the scene editor.

- **Scene Info** — name, description, and the **Home Page** picker
- **Focused layout** — a scaffold, not a filter. Read the warning below before
  you touch it. Not available on Core-First.
- **Pages (N)** — every page, with a **HOME** badge on the home page and tile
  counts. Orange and red chips flag pages with words needing review.
- **Add Page** — see [Pages and navigation](pages-and-navigation.md)
- **Eye icon** (toolbar) — preview the scene exactly as the child sees it
- **Sparkle icon** — refine the whole scene with AI
- **Share icon** — export it. A scene carries its author, so what you send
  arrives labelled "by <your name>" rather than as an anonymous file — which
  matters when a therapist's board is passed between a family, a classroom aide
  and a school device, and someone needs to know whose it is. Set the name once
  in Admin → Device and it travels with everything you share from then on.

**If new words need pictures**, a **New-Word Art** section appears at the top
with a count and a Generate button. Art generation runs in the background and
survives leaving the editor. **Generate all styles** makes art for every tile
style rather than just the active one, so the scene still looks right if you or
a family switch styles later.

The arithmetic is worth knowing, because artwork is where AI spend actually
goes: **about 4¢ per picture**, so roughly **20¢ per word** with all five
styles on. A scene introducing thirty new words is a little over a dollar in
one style and about six in five. Leave it off unless you know the board will be
seen in more than one style.

---

## A warning about Focused layout

**Set it once, early, and then leave it alone.**

Focused layout is not a view filter. It is a scaffold: switching it rebuilds
the scene's structure around the words on your **home page** — a short needs
strip (hungry/thirsty, help, feelings) plus a link out to body & health when
it's on, the fuller set of category pages when it's off.

The consequence is the part to be careful about. **Pages you added yourself do
not survive the toggle.** The rebuild starts from the home page's words, so a
feelings page or a snack page you built by hand is not carried across — and
switching back does not bring it back. On a scene you have only just generated
there is nothing to lose; on one you have spent an afternoon on, there is.

So: decide focused or full while the scene is new, and after that treat the
toggle as off-limits. If you want fewer words in front of a child on a given
day, **conceal** them on the page instead — that keeps every other word exactly
where the child learned it, and it is reversible.

**Refine has the same edge.** Refine is good at adding and rewording, and blunt
about removal: asking it to "remove the core words and body health" can take
new words with it, because it is regenerating the scene rather than editing
your copy of it. Remove things by hand in the editor, where you can see what
went.

We think this is the wrong shape for the feature and expect to change it — a
scaffold you opt into once should not be a switch that can quietly cost you
work. Until it does change, the guidance above is the safe path.

## Making the child actually see it

Creating a scene doesn't activate it. Go to **Admin → Scenes** and either tap
**Activate** on the scene's row or swipe right on it — the swipe is the
shortcut, the button is the one people actually find.

Then check your work the way the child will meet it: leave Admin, and use the
scene. Preview is good; the real grid on the real device is better.
